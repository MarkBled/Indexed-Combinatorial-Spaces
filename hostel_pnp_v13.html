Conflict rules: students who must not share a room

Each line is one forbidden pair, e.g. St-1-B, St-2-B. Only same-gender pairs matter (different genders never share a room anyway).
With conflicts, some indices become invalid. The question is how to reach a valid index without trying them one by one. Two methods are offered below:
  • Naive scan: try index+1, index+2, … The last digits change only the last rooms, so a conflict in an early room survives an astronomically long run of indices.
  • Block skip (backtracking): find the first room that contains a conflict and jump over the whole block of indices that share that room. This is classic backtracking search expressed with indices: fast when conflicts are sparse, exponential in the worst case.

Small exact lab: where counting becomes hard

Removing forbidden pairs from the pair triangle is easy: the triangle has n(n−1)/2 − k allowed pairs. Hard is the next step: how many complete arrangements can be built only from allowed pairs. Exact unranking needs exactly these counts (the block sizes), and they are no longer equal for every branch.
  • Pairs only (perfect matchings avoiding forbidden pairs): checking that one exists is polynomial (Edmonds' algorithm), but counting them is #P-complete in general (Valiant 1979).
  • Rooms of 4 with forbidden pairs inside a room: even deciding whether one valid arrangement exists is NP-complete in general (partition into groups of a fixed size ≥ 3).
